Building software for office environments is one thing. Building it for underground utility tunnels, live industrial facilities, and multi-site construction crews is something else entirely. Connectivity drops. GPS fails indoors. Workers are gloved, moving, and cannot retry a failed request.

And every missed record — a check-in, a signed permit, a corrective action — is not just a bug. It is a compliance gap, a payroll dispute, or a safety audit that cannot close.

In this podcast, Sidra Irshad (Senior Development Manager, Xgrid) and Anees Iqbal (Senior Technical Lead, Xgrid) walk through how they modernised the workforce management system for a large North American construction contractor using Temporal — covering five production use cases and the engineering decisions behind each one.

Session Breakdown
What You Will Learn in This Session
Five production use cases dissected by the engineers who built and shipped them. Each section covers the problem, the architectural decision, and the production outcome — with no hand-waving.
01 Modelling the Worker Shift as a Single Durable Workflow Prevents data drift +
How to represent a complete worker shift — from clock-in to sign-off — as a single long-running workflow that survives connectivity loss, app restarts, and server failures. The workflow owns the lifecycle and ensures every event is accounted for in the correct sequence, without relying on external coordination.
02 Handling Burst Events and Offline Sync Solves reconnection chaos +
What happens when a worker returns to connectivity after working offline — and forty-seven buffered events arrive at once, out of order. How to handle burst ingestion without creating coherence gaps or duplicate records, and how Temporal signals provide ordered, exactly-once processing for each event batch.
03 Document Signing with Multi-Signatory Coordination No manual chasing +
Managing permit-to-work and safety-critical documents that require sign-off from multiple parties across different locations and roles. How to orchestrate parallel signature requests, handle partial completions, and ensure the workflow only advances when all required conditions are met — without polling or manual follow-up.
04 Safety Inspection Escalation with Evidence Validation Real-time vs cron +
How to replace a fifteen-minute cron escalation loop with a parallel-timer workflow that escalates through supervisors, managers, and safety officers simultaneously — and cancels immediately the moment any action is taken on any channel, without the rest of the timers continuing to fire.
05 Notification Orchestration Across Channels One source of truth +
Delivering time-sensitive notifications across push, SMS, and email in a way that tracks delivery state, handles channel failures gracefully, and avoids duplicate notifications when the same message goes out via multiple routes. How to build a single notification workflow that owns the entire delivery lifecycle.

The Bottom Line

Distributed complexity does not disappear when you ignore it. This podcast is for engineering teams building systems where conditions are not ideal, failure has real consequences, and the answer is giving complexity a proper home with a runtime that survives the environment you are actually operating in.
